Implant

R24R anatomical implant

18 cases

Indication

Breast augmentation for women with little breast tissue who want a contour with a gentle slope in the upper pole. The implant is teardrop shaped and placed with the R24R protocol, which favors early movement and a quicker return to routine.

What can influence recovery

  • Skin thickness and amount of breast tissue
  • Pre-existing chest and breast asymmetries
  • Smoking and individual healing
  • Following rest, bra and physiotherapy guidance

Possible complications

  • Capsular contracture
  • Implant rotation, specific to the anatomical shape
  • Seroma, hematoma or infection
  • Changes in sensation
  • Residual asymmetry
Implant

R24R round implant

14 cases

Indication

Breast augmentation for women who want more fullness in the upper pole. The round implant has no fixed orientation and is also placed with the R24R protocol.

What can influence recovery

  • Skin thickness and amount of breast tissue
  • Chest width and breast base
  • Smoking and individual healing
  • Following rest, bra and physiotherapy guidance

Possible complications

  • Capsular contracture
  • Palpable or visible rippling in thin skin
  • Seroma, hematoma or infection
  • Changes in sensation
  • Residual asymmetry
Mastopexy

T mastopexy

12 cases

Indication

Marked sagging with excess skin, common after pregnancy, breastfeeding or weight loss. The breast is lifted and reshaped, with or without an implant, and the scar runs around the areola, vertically and along the breast fold.

What can influence recovery

  • Skin quality and elasticity
  • Weight changes and future pregnancies
  • Smoking and individual healing
  • Bra use and scar care

Possible complications

  • Widening or thickening of the scar
  • Wound opening, especially where the T meets
  • Changes in areola sensation
  • Residual asymmetry
  • Sagging again over time
Mastopexy

L mastopexy

8 cases

Indication

Moderate sagging, when the excess skin can be removed with a shorter L-shaped scar around the areola, vertically and along part of the fold. It can be done with or without an implant.

What can influence recovery

  • Degree of sagging and amount of skin
  • Skin quality and elasticity
  • Weight changes and future pregnancies
  • Smoking and individual healing

Possible complications

  • Widening or thickening of the scar
  • Wound opening
  • Changes in areola sensation
  • Residual asymmetry
  • Sagging again over time
Correction

Tuberous breast correction

12 cases

Indication

A congenital condition in which the breast base is narrow, the fold sits high and the areola tends to protrude. The correction releases and redistributes the base, repositions the fold and, in most cases, adds an implant. The areola may be reduced.

What can influence recovery

  • Degree of the condition in each breast
  • Volume difference between sides
  • Amount of skin and tissue
  • Individual healing

Possible complications

  • Residual asymmetry
  • Double-bubble contour
  • Capsular contracture
  • Widening of the areola scar
  • Changes in sensation
Revision

Implant revision

4 cases

Indication

Replacing the implant because of capsular contracture, rupture, displacement or the wish to change volume. It can be combined with mastopexy when the breast has sagged.

What can influence recovery

  • Condition of the capsule and tissues around the previous implant
  • Skin quality
  • Age and type of the previous implant
  • Individual healing

Possible complications

  • New capsular contracture
  • Seroma, hematoma or infection
  • Changes in sensation
  • Residual asymmetry
  • Widening of the scar
Reduction

Breast reduction

25 cases

Indication

Excessive breast volume causing back and shoulder pain, bra strap marks or limiting activities. Part of the tissue is removed and the breast is reshaped, usually with a T scar. In some cases the patient's own tissue is used to fill the upper pole, a technique called autoprosthesis.

What can influence recovery

  • Body weight and weight changes
  • Future pregnancies
  • Smoking and individual healing
  • Scar care and bra use

Possible complications

  • Widening or thickening of the scar
  • Wound opening where the T meets
  • Changes in areola sensation
  • Possible difficulty breastfeeding
  • Residual asymmetry

About these images

The images show patients operated on by Dr. Diego Viégas and were published with written consent. No patient is identified, and the areolas are covered to protect their privacy.

Each image shows the preoperative state on the left and the postoperative recovery on the right. The time after surgery varies between cases.

Recovery depends on anatomy, age, skin and tissue quality, healing and postoperative care. The images are educational and do not represent a promise of results. Any procedure is only indicated after a consultation.
